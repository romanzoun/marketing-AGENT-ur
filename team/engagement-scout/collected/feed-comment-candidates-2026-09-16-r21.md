🤖💡 How can an AI agent remain trusted after its initial identity and authorization checks?

To answer this critical question, Ant Group, Tsinghua University, the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology (CAICT), and GLEIF have developed a joint research paper under the collaboration framework of IIFAA. The paper was launched today at the 2026 Inclusion Conference on the Bund.

The paper proposes a framework that connects four key capabilities for moving from one-time authentication to continuous trust:
- Structured identity attributes that establish who the agent is, whom it belongs to, and what it can do
- Continuous trust assessment based on identity, environment, behavior, and context
- Cross-domain recognition that allows necessary identity and authorization facts to be verified across organizations and platforms
- Continuous monitoring that feeds changes, anomalies, and outcomes back into trust assessments and authorization policies

This framework also distinguishes among an agent’s base identity, the identity of the specific runtime instance, and the responsible-party identity that establishes whom the agent represents and what authority it has.

This is where the Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) and verifiable LEI (vLEI) fit in. The LEI anchors the verified identity of the legal entity delegating the task, while the vLEI enables computational verification of the organizational role and authority of the person issuing that delegation. Together, they provide an identity and authority foundation for establishing who is authorized to commit an organization when an agent acts on its behalf.
