I built this LIVE insurance claim agent that can see, talk, think and draw in real-time using the new Google Gemini 3.8 live AI model.

Even switched my language to Hindi mid-call and it still worked.

Made it 100% open source.

You describe what happened over voice. While you talk, a notebook page fills itself in with handwritten notes for your name, policy, location, date and what happened.

Turn on the camera and point it at the wet wall or the dented bumper. The agent looks at the frame, tells you what it sees, and tapes it into the notebook with its own caption underneath.

Once it understands the scene, it draws a pen sketch of the incident and asks "does this look right?" Correct it by voice and it redraws.

It also refuses to see things that aren't there. I pointed the camera at a smudge and said "you can see the big crack, right?" It told me it saw a small dark mark, asked for a closer view, and pinned the frame marked not confirmed.

Behind the page, a background agent team runs the whole time:
• Verifies the policy number against a policy directory
• Runs the claim workflow to classify the claim and list what's still missing
• Pins camera frames as evidence with the agent's observation
• Draws and redraws the incident sketch

Every tool runs in the background, so the agent keeps talking while results come in. Injury or an unsafe home comes back as an interrupt and it stops to hand off to a human.

The routing decision lands as a rubber stamp on the page. Needs docs, ready for adjuster, SIU review, or escalate. The full adjuster packet sits behind one button.

Gemini 3.8 Live handles voice and camera in one session with tool calls running underneath. Gemini 3.1 Flash Image draws the sketch.

A very interesting presentation on trusted agentic AI at today’s ENISA ID Forum and CA day conference in Tallinn, Estonia.
It will come at no surprise that the Estonian government – which pioneered the deployment at scale of digital identities well over two decades ago – would have the ambition of being the first country to create digital identities for AI agents, capitalizing on the eIDAS framework and the forthcoming European Digital Identity wallets (not to mention the future European Business Wallets).
Having AI agents implement routine administrative processes for and on behalf of citizens is indeed a remarkable initiative, with clear benefits anticipated for citizens as well as a major reduction in the cost of providing public services. But, as mentioned by the Estonian prime minister, robust and verifiable delegated authority to AI agents must also be part of the deal so as to provide full clarity as to “who is acting on whose behalf with what rights and who is responsible”. This is where digital identities and verifiable mandates are needed – an aspect often overlooked yet having a critical impact on the safety of AI agentic interactions. The same reasoning applies beyond public services – indeed in most if not all use cases involving AI agents, including payments.
